## Research Overview

My doctoral research at the **Indian Institute of Technology Delhi** focused on developing physics-based and data-driven computational frameworks for understanding and optimizing charged aerosol particle transport and collection.

The research combined **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), electrostatics, Lagrangian particle tracking, multiphysics modeling, machine learning, explainable artificial intelligence, surrogate modeling, and multi-objective optimization**.

A central objective of the work was to bridge high-fidelity numerical simulations with modern machine learning methodologies to accelerate engineering analysis while retaining physical interpretability.

## Multiphysics Modeling

Developed three-dimensional multiphysics numerical models in **COMSOL Multiphysics** by coupling:

- Computational Fluid Dynamics
- Electrostatics
- Lagrangian Particle Tracking
- Particle–collector interactions

The numerical framework was used to investigate charged particle transport and deposition around spherical collectors under varying operating conditions.

The influence of parameters including particle diameter, flow velocity, applied voltage, particle charge, and collector geometry was systematically investigated.

## Particle Dynamics and Collection

Investigated the physical mechanisms governing charged particle transport and collection, including:

- Hydrodynamic drag
- Electrostatic forces
- Particle trajectories
- Oblique particle impacts
- Particle rebound
- Collision efficiency
- Rebound fraction
- Overall collection efficiency

The analysis provided mechanistic insight into how competing hydrodynamic and electrostatic effects influence particle capture.

## Scientific Machine Learning

Developed machine learning surrogate models to approximate computationally expensive multiphysics simulations and enable rapid exploration of large engineering design spaces.

The work included:

- Surrogate modeling
- Gradient boosting models
- LightGBM
- Hyperparameter optimization
- Large-scale surrogate prediction
- Data-driven analysis of simulation results

## Explainable Artificial Intelligence

Applied **Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)** techniques to interpret machine learning predictions and connect data-driven results with underlying physical mechanisms.

Methods included:

- SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
- Partial Dependence Plots (PDP)
- Feature importance analysis
- Parameter sensitivity analysis

These approaches were used to identify dominant operating parameters and favorable conditions for improving particle collection while suppressing rebound.

## Surrogate-Assisted Optimization

Developed a data-driven multi-objective optimization framework integrating:

- LightGBM surrogate modeling
- K-means clustering
- NSGA-II
- TOPSIS
- Explainable AI

K-means clustering was used to identify favorable regions of the design space and provide physically meaningful bounds for subsequent optimization.

NSGA-II was then employed to determine Pareto-optimal trade-offs between maximizing particle collision efficiency and minimizing particle rebound.

## Research Outcomes

The doctoral research resulted in peer-reviewed research publications and computational frameworks spanning **multiphysics simulation, particle dynamics, interpretable machine learning, surrogate modeling, and engineering optimization**.

The work established a computational workflow progressing from:

**Physics-Based Modeling → High-Fidelity Simulation → Machine Learning → Explainable AI → Surrogate-Assisted Optimization**

This research provided the foundation for my subsequent work in **Scientific Machine Learning, Bayesian Inference, Bayesian Inverse Problems, and Uncertainty Quantification**.
